<h2 style="margin:0 0 20px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#0a0a0a;letter-spacing:-0.3px;">Cloud Services &amp; Major Provider Deals</h2>

<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;color:#374151;">
<strong>AWS/Amazon</strong> scored a major deal with <strong>Meta</strong>, which signed on to use millions of <strong>AWS Graviton</strong> ARM-based CPUs to power AI agent workloads (real-time reasoning, code generation, multi-step task coordination). This strategically pulls Meta's spending back toward AWS after Meta's prior $10B six-year deal with <strong>Google Cloud</strong> last August. Meta also uses <strong>Microsoft Azure</strong>. AWS timed the announcement to coincide with the end of <strong>Google Cloud Next</strong>. Separately, <strong>Anthropic</strong> committed $100B over 10 years to run workloads on AWS, focusing on Amazon's <strong>Trainium</strong> custom AI chips, while Amazon invested another $5B into Anthropic (totaling $13B).
</p>

<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;color:#374151;">
<strong>Google</strong> announced sweeping <strong>Workspace</strong> AI updates at Cloud Next, including "Workspace Intelligence" (an AI system drawing on Gmail, Calendar, Chat, Drive), Gemini-powered Google Sheets automation, and AI writing tools for Docs. Google also makes its own custom AI chips and unveiled new versions at the conference. Google is projected to spend $175B–$185B in capex, and is reportedly planning to invest up to <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/366c73dd-4006-4ce6-9816-5004447d30b8" style="color:#2563eb;text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid #2563eb;"><strong>$40B in Anthropic</strong></a>.
</p>

<p style="margin:0 0 0 0;font-size:15px;color:#374151;">
<a href="https://www.ft.com/content/f5776fd6-22f3-43d1-806b-9858b64cfd18" style="color:#2563eb;text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid #2563eb;"><strong>Microsoft</strong></a> is offering voluntary redundancy to 7% of its US staff for the first time, amid shifts in its workforce as AI reshapes priorities.
