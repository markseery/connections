## Summary

The AI industry is experiencing massive infrastructure investment, fierce competition among cloud providers, and rapid model development across the ecosystem.

### Cloud & Infrastructure

**AWS/Amazon** is navigating dual investments in competing AI labs. AWS CEO Matt Garman defended Amazon's $50 billion investment in OpenAI alongside its existing $8 billion commitment to Anthropic, arguing that AWS has long managed partner-competitor relationships. Separately, **Uber expanded its AWS contract**, adopting Amazon's Graviton CPUs and trialing Trainium3 AI chips—a notable shift given Uber's prior multi-year cloud commitments to **Oracle and Google Cloud**. Jeff Bezos's **Project Prometheus** also poached an xAI co-founder from OpenAI, signaling Amazon's broader AI ambitions.

**Anthropic** signed an expanded compute deal with **Google Cloud and Broadcom** for increased TPU capacity (reportedly 3.5 gigawatts per a Broadcom SEC filing), coming online in 2027. This extends Anthropic's $50 billion U.S. compute infrastructure commitment. Anthropic also previewed its new frontier model **Mythos** through "Project Glasswing," a cybersecurity initiative with partners including **Amazon, Apple, Microsoft, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike**, and others. The model identified thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities. Anthropic recently closed a $30 billion Series G at a $380 billion valuation. The **UK is actively courting Anthropic** to expand in London following its clash with the U.S. Defense Department, which labeled it a supply-chain risk.

**Google** quietly launched "AI Edge Eloquent," an offline-first dictation app on iOS using Gemma-based models, and added Gemini-powered AI photo captioning to Google Maps.

**Microsoft** launched a "mid-class" AI model as compute limits bite, reflecting growing pressure to offer efficient alternatives amid constrained GPU supply.

**Meta** released its first AI model since Zuckerberg's massive spending spree.

**Firmus**, a Singapore-based AI data center provider backed by **Nvidia**, raised $505 million at a $5.5 billion valuation (led by Coatue), bringing total fundraising to $1.35 billion in six months. Its "Project Southgate" is building energy-efficient AI data centers in Australia using Nvidia's next-gen **Vera Rubin** platform. Originally a Bitcoin mining cooling company, Firmus is the latest crypto-to-AI pivot.

**Intel** joined Elon Musk's **Terafab** semiconductor project alongside SpaceX and Tesla, providing the chip fabrication expertise those companies lack. The factory aims to produce AI and robotics chips in Texas.

**Samsung** forecast record profits driven by the AI boom.

Insurers are turning to **catastrophe bonds** to offload growing data center risks, reflecting the sector's rapid physical expansion.

### AI Models & Startups

**Arcee**, a 26-person startup, released its open-source 400B-parameter reasoning model "Trinity Large Thinking" on a $20 million budget, positioning it as a Western alternative to capable Chinese models and a popular choice for OpenClaw users after Anthropic restricted Claude access.

**OpenAI** released a child safety blueprint developed with NCMEC, acquired a popular tech talk show, and continues expanding its ChatGPT app ecosystem—**Tubi** became the first streaming service to launch a native app within ChatGPT.

**Databricks** co-founder Matei Zaharia won the ACM Prize in Computing, declaring "AGI is here already" while the company hits $5.4 billion revenue run rate at a $134 billion valuation.

**Atlassian** launched visual AI tools (Remix) and third-party agents in Confluence using MCPs, connecting to Lovable, Replit, and Gamma.

### Investment & Market Trends

Private wealth and family offices are increasingly investing directly in AI startups, bypassing VCs. Arena Private Wealth co-led a $230 million round in AI chip startup **Positron**. The AI investment frenzy is drawing overlapping investors across competing companies—Anthropic's $30 billion round included multiple OpenAI backers, including Microsoft.
