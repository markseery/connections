## Summary

### OpenAI: Restructuring, Acquisitions & Product Battles

OpenAI is undergoing significant internal shifts. It acquired personal finance startup **Hiro** and new media company **TBPN** — small acqui-hires aimed at finding sticky consumer products beyond chatbots and improving its public image. Key executives departed: **Kevin Weil** (who led OpenAI for Science), **Bill Peebles** (creator of Sora, the AI video tool shut down after losing ~$1M/day in compute), and CTO of enterprise apps **Srinivas Narayanan**. The company is consolidating around enterprise AI and a forthcoming "superapp," shedding "side quests." OpenAI revamped **Codex**, its AI coding tool, adding background agentic capabilities on Mac desktops — a direct response to **Anthropic's Claude Code**, which is widely seen as winning the enterprise coding battle. OpenAI also released **GPT-Rosalind** for life sciences research.

### Anthropic: Mythos, Government Relations & New Products

**Anthropic** unveiled its **Mythos** model, described as too powerful for public release but demonstrated to Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Despite being designated a Pentagon supply-chain risk, Anthropic CEO **Dario Amodei** met with White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and Bessent in a "productive" meeting, signaling a thawing relationship with the Trump administration. The FT reports Mythos is testing the limits of global cyber defenses, with financial officials warning latest AI models could threaten the world banking system. A UK minister said companies "should be worried" about the model's capabilities. Anthropic also launched **Claude Design**, a visual prototyping tool powered by Claude Opus 4.7 for Pro/Max/Team/Enterprise subscribers, with Canva export integration — targeting non-designers who need quick mockups.

### Cerebras Systems: IPO & AWS/OpenAI Deals

AI chip startup **Cerebras Systems** filed to go public (IPO planned for mid-May), after a previous 2024 filing was withdrawn. The company reached a **$23 billion valuation** after a $1B Series H in February. Cerebras announced an agreement with **Amazon Web Services (AWS)** to use its chips in Amazon data centers, plus a deal with OpenAI reportedly worth **$10B+**. CEO Andrew Feldman claimed Cerebras took the "fast inference business at OpenAI" from **Nvidia**. Revenue was $510M in 2025 with $237.8M net income.

### AI Infrastructure & Data Centers

The **FT** reports that **data center delays threaten to choke AI expansion**, a critical bottleneck for cloud providers and AI companies. Data center startup **Fluidstack** is positioning itself for frontier labs, including a reported **$50B agreement with Anthropic**. **Upscale AI**, a 7-month-old AI infrastructure company building custom chips, is in talks to raise $180-200M at a ~$2B valuation (investors include Tiger Global, Premji Invest) — despite having no product yet. AI infrastructure startup **Recursive** raised **$500M** for self-teaching AI.

### AI Coding Startups: Cursor & Factory

**Cursor** is in talks to raise **$2B+ at a $50B valuation** (led by Thrive and Andreessen Horowitz, with Nvidia participating). It forecasts ending 2026 with **$6B+ annualized revenue**, tripling from February's $2B run rate. It recently achieved slight gross margin profitability via a proprietary model and cheaper third-party models. **Factory** raised $150M at a $1.5B valuation (Khosla, Sequoia, Blackstone) for enterprise AI coding agents. Meanwhile, a phenomenon called **"tokenmaxxing"** — developers bragging about massive AI token budgets — is proving counterproductive: real-world code acceptance rates drop to 10-30% after revision churn.

### Autonomous Vehicles & Mobility

**Tesla** expanded its robotaxi service to **Dallas and Houston** (both driverless), now operating in three Texas cities after Austin. Data shows very few vehicles deployed so far (1 each in Dallas/Houston vs. 46 in Austin). **Uber** has committed **$10B+** to autonomous vehicles — $2.5B in direct investments and $7.5B for purchasing robotaxis — with stakes in WeRide, Lucid, Nuro, Rivian, and Wayve. Chipmakers **AMD, Arm, and Qualcomm** invested $60M in UK self-driving startup **Wayve**.

### Other Notable Developments

- **Sam Altman's World** (formerly Worldcoin) is expanding iris-scanning human verification to **Tinder**, event ticketing, email, and enterprise, positioning "proof of human" tools against AI-generated identity fraud.
- **App Store releases surged 60% YoY** in Q1 2026 (80% on iOS alone), likely driven by AI making app development more accessible — contradicting predictions that AI would kill apps.
- **Palantir** published a 22-point ideological manifesto from CEO Alex Karp's book, amid scrutiny over its ICE surveillance work and positioning as defending "the West."
- **Physical Intelligence** published research on its **π0.7** model showing robots performing tasks never explicitly trained on (compositional generalization), suggesting robotics AI may be nearing an LLM-like inflection point.
- **Luma AI** launched **Innovative Dreams**, an AI production studio with Wonder Project, debuting "The Old Stories: Moses" starring Ben Kingsley on **Amazon Prime Video**.
- **Crypto and AI PACs** raised **$250M** ahead of US midterm elections.
- **China** is probing **Meta's $2B Manus deal** and seeing surging chip tool imports from Southeast Asia.
- The **AI boom** is described as potentially "massively disinflationary" by Northern Trust.
- Elad Gil advised AI startup founders that most companies have a **~12-month window** at peak value before it crashes — urging pre-scheduled board discussions about exits.
