<h1>CoreWeave's Explosive April &amp; the AI Infrastructure Funding Boom</h1>
<p class="subtitle">Weekly AI Infrastructure Briefing</p>
</div>

<div class="content">

<h2>CoreWeave's Major Deal Spree</h2>

<p><strong>CoreWeave (CRWV)</strong> dominated AI cloud infrastructure news in April 2026 with three consecutive blockbuster deals:</p>

<div class="deal-card">
<p class="deal-title">1. Meta Platforms — $21B Expansion</p>
<p>Meta committed an additional $21 billion to CoreWeave's AI cloud infrastructure running from 2027 to 2032, on top of a prior $14.2 billion arrangement, bringing total commitments to <strong>over $35 billion</strong>. Meta, which guided $115–$135 billion in 2026 capex, needs external capacity beyond its own data centers to meet AI compute demand.</p>
</div>

<blockquote><p>"There's just too much risk not to." — CoreWeave CEO Mike Intrator</p></blockquote>

<div class="deal-card">
<p class="deal-title">2. Anthropic — Multi-Year Agreement</p>
<p>CoreWeave signed a multi-year production deal with Anthropic to power its Claude AI models — a new customer logo. With this addition, <strong>nine of the ten leading AI model providers</strong> now use CoreWeave's platform (only xAI is excluded). Anthropic also released its Claude Opus 4.7 model and is expanding its London operations with an 800-person office to serve UK banks through "Project Glasswing."</p>
</div>

<div class="deal-card">
<p class="deal-title">3. Jane Street — $7B Total Commitment</p>
<p>The quantitative trading firm committed ~$6 billion to use CoreWeave's AI cloud platform and made a separate $1 billion equity investment at $109/share, making it one of CoreWeave's five largest shareholders. The deal gives Jane Street access to <strong>Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips</strong> across multiple CoreWeave data centers, extending CoreWeave's reach into quantitative finance workloads.</p>
</div>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>CoreWeave's Aggressive Capital Raising</h2>

<p>CoreWeave simultaneously pursued multiple financing avenues:</p>

<ul class="bullet-list">
<li>Raised ~$3.5 billion in <strong>convertible senior notes</strong></li>
<li>Issued <strong>$1.75 billion in high-yield bonds</strong>, followed by an additional <strong>$1 billion reopening</strong> of 9.75% senior notes due 2031</li>
<li>Secured an <strong>$8.5 billion delayed draw term loan</strong> at investment-grade ratings (its first)</li>
<li>Upsized a high-yield bond offering due to heavy demand</li>
</ul>

<p>CoreWeave's stock surged approximately <strong>190% over the past year</strong>, trading near $118. The company reported 2025 revenue of $5.13 billion, though it posted a $1.17 billion net loss. It operates <strong>43 active data centers</strong>.</p>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>Analyst Actions on CoreWeave</h2>

<ul class="bullet-list">
<li><strong>Bernstein (SocGen Group)</strong> raised its price target to $67 from $56 but maintained an <strong>Underperform</strong> rating, citing longer-term concerns despite strong near-term momentum.</li>
<li>CoreWeave's stock surged on an analyst upgrade, joining the Nasdaq-100 conversation alongside Sandisk.</li>
<li>CoreWeave CEO Michael Intrator sold 307,693 shares ($113–$119/share) under a pre-planned Rule 10b5-1 trading plan while retaining substantial holdings.</li>
</ul>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>Google-Linked Data Center Bond Sale</h2>

<p><strong>Google</strong>-backed data centers sold a record <strong>$5.7 billion junk bond</strong> offering led by Morgan Stanley, attracting $19 billion in investor orders. Combined with CoreWeave's $1 billion add-on, the two raised <strong>$6.7 billion in new junk debt</strong> — underscoring the AI financing frenzy.</p>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>Nvidia's Role</h2>

<p><strong>Nvidia</strong> is the primary beneficiary of AI infrastructure spending, as CoreWeave's data centers run entirely on Nvidia GPUs. Nvidia holds a $6.3 billion strategic collaboration with CoreWeave and was named Nvidia Exemplar Cloud.</p>

<blockquote><p>CEO Jensen Huang admitted on a podcast that Nvidia was "late to moving in backing foundational AI laboratories like Anthropic," as AI labs required $5–$10 billion in funding beyond traditional VC models. Nvidia now says it is "delighted to invest" in OpenAI and Anthropic.</p></blockquote>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>TSMC Earnings Signal Strong AI Demand</h2>

<p><strong>Taiwan Semiconductor (TSMC)</strong> reported Q1 earnings with a <strong>58% profit increase</strong> and <strong>35% revenue growth</strong>, with CEO comments signaling robust and sustained AI chip demand.</p>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>Applied Digital (APLD) and CoreWeave</h2>

<p><strong>Applied Digital</strong> saw stock movement tied to amended lease terms with CoreWeave and progress on its Delta Forge 1 data center buildout. Its HPC hosting business achieved a <strong>24.7% operating margin</strong> in Q3, with CoreWeave as a key tenant.</p>

<hr class="divider">

<h2>Broader Market Context</h2>

<p>The neocloud sector is emerging as its own asset class. Morningstar analyst Luke Yang noted that cloud infrastructure companies are using every available financing tool — delayed draw term loans, corporate bonds, convertible notes, OEM financing, and leases — with creditors becoming increasingly comfortable with the neocloud business model.</p>

<p>Tech giants' <strong>$630+ billion in planned 2026 capex</strong> remains insufficient to meet AI compute demand, creating opportunity for specialized providers like CoreWeave.</p>
