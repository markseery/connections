# Summary: CoreWeave's Explosive April and the AI Infrastructure Funding Boom

## CoreWeave's Major Deal Spree

**CoreWeave (CRWV)** dominated AI cloud infrastructure news in April 2026 with three consecutive blockbuster deals:

1. **Meta Platforms ($21B expansion):** Meta committed an additional $21 billion to CoreWeave's AI cloud infrastructure running from 2027 to 2032, on top of a prior $14.2 billion arrangement, bringing total commitments to over $35 billion. Meta, which guided $115–$135 billion in 2026 capex, needs external capacity beyond its own data centers to meet AI compute demand. CoreWeave CEO Mike Intrator noted: "There's just too much risk not to."

2. **Anthropic (multi-year agreement):** CoreWeave signed a multi-year production deal with Anthropic to power its Claude AI models. This marked a new customer logo for CoreWeave. With this addition, nine of the ten leading AI model providers now use CoreWeave's platform (only xAI is excluded). Anthropic also released its Claude Opus 4.7 model and is expanding its London operations with an 800-person office to serve UK banks through "Project Glasswing."

3. **Jane Street ($7B total commitment):** The quantitative trading firm committed ~$6 billion to use CoreWeave's AI cloud platform and made a separate $1 billion equity investment at $109/share, making it one of CoreWeave's five largest shareholders. The deal gives Jane Street access to **Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips** across multiple CoreWeave data centers, extending CoreWeave's reach into quantitative finance workloads.

## CoreWeave's Aggressive Capital Raising

CoreWeave simultaneously pursued multiple financing avenues:
- Raised ~$3.5 billion in **convertible senior notes**
- Issued **$1.75 billion in high-yield bonds**, followed by an additional **$1 billion reopening** of 9.75% senior notes due 2031
- Secured an **$8.5 billion delayed draw term loan** at investment-grade ratings (its first)
- Upsized a high-yield bond offering due to heavy demand

CoreWeave's stock surged over **50% in April** and approximately **190% over the past year**, trading near $118. The company carries a **$66.8 billion revenue backlog**, with 2025 revenue of $5.13 billion (up 168% YoY), though it posted a $1.17 billion net loss. It operates 43 active data centers.

## Analyst Actions on CoreWeave

- **Bernstein (SocGen Group)** raised its price target to $67 from $56 but maintained an **Underperform** rating, citing longer-term concerns despite strong near-term momentum.
- CoreWeave's stock surged on an analyst upgrade, joining the Nasdaq-100 conversation alongside Sandisk.
- CoreWeave CEO Michael Intrator sold 307,693 shares ($113–$119/share) under a pre-planned Rule 10b5-1 trading plan while retaining substantial holdings.

## Google-Linked Data Center Bond Sale

**Google**-backed data centers sold a record **$5.7 billion junk bond** offering led by Morgan Stanley, attracting $19 billion in investor orders. Combined with CoreWeave's $1 billion add-on, the two raised **$6.7 billion in new junk debt** — underscoring the AI financing frenzy.

## Nvidia's Role

**Nvidia** is the primary beneficiary of AI infrastructure spending, as CoreWeave's data centers run entirely on Nvidia GPUs. Nvidia holds a $6.3 billion strategic collaboration with CoreWeave and was named Nvidia Exemplar Cloud. CEO Jensen Huang admitted on a podcast that Nvidia was "late to moving in backing foundational AI laboratories like Anthropic," as AI labs required $5–$10 billion in funding beyond traditional VC models. Nvidia now says it is "delighted to invest" in OpenAI and Anthropic.

## TSMC Earnings Signal Strong AI Demand

**Taiwan Semiconductor (TSMC)** reported Q1 earnings with a **58% profit increase** and **35% revenue growth**, with CEO comments signaling robust and sustained AI chip demand.

## Applied Digital (APLD) and CoreWeave

**Applied Digital** saw stock movement tied to amended lease terms with CoreWeave and progress on its Delta Forge 1 data center buildout. Its HPC hosting business achieved a 24.7% operating margin in Q3, with CoreWeave as a key tenant.

## Broader Market Context

The neocloud sector is emerging as its own asset class. Morningstar analyst Luke Yang noted that cloud infrastructure companies are using every available financing tool — delayed draw term loans, corporate bonds, convertible notes, OEM financing, and leases — with creditors becoming increasingly comfortable with the neocloud business model. Tech giants' $630+ billion in planned 2026 capex remains insufficient to meet AI compute demand, creating opportunity for specialized providers like CoreWeave.
