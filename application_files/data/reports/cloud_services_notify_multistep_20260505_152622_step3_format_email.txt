<div class="header">
<p>AI Infrastructure Briefing</p>
<h1>CoreWeave Dominates AI Cloud Infrastructure Headlines</h1>
</div>

<div class="content">

<div class="section">
<h2>Major Deals &amp; Partnerships</h2>
<p><strong>CoreWeave</strong> is at the center of a massive AI infrastructure buildout, having secured several landmark agreements:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Meta</strong> committed ~$21 billion in expanded AI cloud capacity through 2032, covering dedicated capacity across multiple sites and featuring early rollouts of <strong>Nvidia's Vera Rubin</strong> platform.</li>
<li><strong>Anthropic</strong> signed a multi-year deal for CoreWeave to power Claude AI models, with computing capacity scheduled to go live later in 2026. This deal is seen as a major validation of CoreWeave's neocloud model.</li>
<li><strong>Jane Street</strong> signed a $6 billion AI cloud agreement with CoreWeave.</li>
<li><strong>OpenAI</strong> has an existing five-year, $11.9 billion contract (including a $350 million private placement giving OpenAI a stake in CoreWeave). Wells Fargo estimates roughly a third of CoreWeave's contracted revenue is tied to OpenAI.</li>
</ul>
<div class="highlight-box">
<p>These deals could push CoreWeave's remaining performance obligations (backlog) past $95 billion, up from ~$66.8 billion at end of 2025.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>Financial Picture</h2>
<p>CoreWeave posted <strong>$5.13 billion in 2025 revenue</strong> (up from $1.92B prior year) but recorded a <strong>$1.17 billion net loss</strong>. The company priced a <strong>$1 billion offering of 9.750% senior notes due 2031</strong> in April 2026 to fund continued data center expansion.</p>
<p>Q1 2026 revenue is projected at ~$1.96 billion. Earnings are scheduled for <strong>May 7</strong> after market close.</p>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>Executive Stock Sales &amp; Insider Activity</h2>
<p>Three senior CoreWeave executives sold <strong>more than $1 billion</strong> in shares in the first four months of 2026 — placing them in the <strong>99th percentile</strong> of S&amp;P 500 insider selling. CEO Michael Intrator alone filed an April 28 sale valued at ~$32.8 million.</p>
<p><strong>Magnetar Financial</strong>, a 10% holder, unloaded ~1.28 million shares on May 1 for ~$154 million and sold call options on 1 million additional shares. The heavy selling has drawn scrutiny from compensation experts, given the company remains unprofitable.</p>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>Analyst Sentiment &amp; Stock Movement</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Jefferies</strong> raised its CoreWeave price target to <strong>$160</strong> (from $120), maintaining Buy, citing leadership in high-performance compute and a multi-trillion-dollar opportunity.</li>
<li><strong>Citi</strong> raised its target to <strong>$155</strong> (from $126), looking for 35–40% quarter-over-quarter backlog growth in Q1.</li>
<li><strong>Goldman Sachs</strong> reiterated Buy on <strong>Apple</strong> (noting App Store deceleration); other analyst calls covered <strong>Amazon, Tesla, Palantir</strong>.</li>
<li>CoreWeave shares rose ~5.4–5.8% ahead of earnings, trading around $125, with a <strong>21.87% short interest</strong> adding volatility potential. The stock remains ~35% below its highs near $183.</li>
<li>Jim Cramer called CoreWeave "aggressive" but with "terrific fundamentals," cautioning about volatility and the possibility of a financing-related surprise.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>Institutional Buying</h2>
<p>New positions were disclosed by the <strong>State of New Jersey Common Pension Fund D</strong> and <strong>Wealthfront Advisers</strong> ($788K position), signaling growing institutional interest.</p>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>Neoclouds vs. Google TPUs</h2>
<p><strong>Nebius, Lambda, and CoreWeave</strong> all stated they are <strong>unlikely to adopt Google's TPUs</strong> any time soon, preferring to stick with <strong>Nvidia GPUs</strong>. All three companies are Nvidia-backed, and they told The Information that Google's custom chips don't fit their current infrastructure strategies.</p>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>CoreWeave Product Developments</h2>
<p>CoreWeave launched <strong>SUNK (SLURM on Kubernetes)</strong> — a platform enabling AI researchers to run SLURM workloads on Kubernetes-based cloud infrastructure. It comes in two versions: SUNK Self-Service (templatized setup) and SUNK Anywhere (multi-cloud). The move targets the growing base of enterprise AI researchers beyond major labs.</p>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2>Broader AI Infrastructure Context</h2>
<ul>
<li>Hyperscaler AI capex is now running <strong>$700–725 billion</strong> across companies like <strong>Microsoft, Google, Amazon/AWS, Oracle</strong>, and others.</li>
<li>OpenAI's reported misses on user growth and revenue targets briefly rattled AI infrastructure stocks including CoreWeave and <strong>Oracle</strong>, though stocks recovered.</li>
<li>The earnings week also features reports from <strong>Arm Holdings, Palantir, Arista, and AppLovin</strong>, with options markets pricing 10–14% post-earnings moves across AI-linked stocks.</li>
</ul>
</div>
