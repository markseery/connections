## Summary: CoreWeave Dominates AI Cloud Infrastructure Headlines Amid Q1 Earnings, Major Deals, and Insider Activity

**CoreWeave** is the central focus across all items, with the company approaching its Q1 2026 earnings report (May 7 after market close). Analysts expect record quarterly revenue of ~$1.96 billion (up from $981.63M a year ago), though the company is still expected to report a per-share loss. The stock has rallied ~103% from its March lows, with shares trading around $127-128, and options traders are pricing in an ~18.7% post-earnings move.

### Major Deals & Business Developments
- **CoreWeave and Meta** expanded their AI cloud partnership to **$21 billion** through December 2032, including early access to Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips.
- **CoreWeave and Anthropic** signed a multi-year infrastructure agreement for Claude AI model deployment, diversifying CoreWeave's customer base beyond its heavy reliance on **Microsoft** (~67% of 2025 revenue).
- **Jane Street** signed a **$6 billion** AI cloud agreement with CoreWeave.
- CoreWeave reported a **$66.8 billion revenue backlog** and guides for $12-13 billion in 2026 revenue with $30-35 billion in capex.
- CoreWeave expanded its **SUNK unified training system** with self-service, SUNK Anywhere, and Mission Control features, positioning the company as an AI orchestration platform (not just a GPU vendor) across multi-cloud and on-premises environments.
- A **$3.1 billion GPU-backed loan** was syndicated to fund expansion, on top of existing 9.75% senior notes.

### Analyst Sentiment
- **Jefferies** raised its price target to **$160** (from $120), citing CoreWeave's leadership in high-performance compute at scale.
- **Bank of America** raised its target to **$140** (from $120), emphasizing data center activation and contracted revenue.
- **Jim Cramer** expects a "big surprise" from CoreWeave's earnings.
- Some analysts are more cautious, with one Simply Wall St narrative valuing the stock at just $70 (suggesting overvaluation), citing heavy debt and leverage risk.

### Insider & Institutional Trading
- CoreWeave's **three most senior executives** sold over **$1 billion** in shares in the first four months of 2026—placing them in the 99th percentile of S&P 500 insider selling.
- **Insider Brian Venturo** sold 375,000 shares.
- **Magnetar Financial** funds conducted massive sell-offs: ~1.16 million shares, ~978,764 shares, ~349,437 shares in separate transactions, plus sold call options on 1 million+ shares at $175 strike and 300,000 shares at $180 strike, and trimmed warrant positions—all while retaining large remaining holdings (~19.3M shares in one account).
- **SBI Securities** holds a $3.29 million position in CoreWeave.

### Neoclouds vs. Google TPUs
- **Nebius, Lambda, and CoreWeave** all stated they are **unlikely to adopt Google's TPUs** anytime soon, preferring to remain committed to **Nvidia GPUs**. All three companies have Nvidia backing, reinforcing their GPU-centric strategies. **Google** had revealed during its earnings call expanded availability of its latest TPU, but the neoclouds see no near-term reason to switch.

### Core Scientific & CoreWeave
- **Core Scientific** reported a $347.2M Q1 loss, sold $208M in Bitcoin, and is pivoting to AI data center operations. Its colocation revenue surged to $77.5M (from $8.6M a year ago), driven primarily by a **590 MW contract expansion with CoreWeave** projected to generate **$10.2 billion in revenue over 12 years**. CoreWeave-related colocation now represents 67% of Core Scientific's total revenue.

### OpenAI Risk
- **OpenAI** reportedly missed recent user and revenue goals, creating concern for CoreWeave since **Wells Fargo** estimates roughly a third of CoreWeave's contracted revenue is tied to OpenAI (which has an $11.9 billion, five-year contract). **Microsoft, Oracle, and Stargate (SoftBank)** are also listed as OpenAI infrastructure partners.

### Community Opposition
- Residents of **Kenilworth, New Jersey** are rallying against CoreWeave's **$1.8 billion, 400,000-sq-ft data center** being built on a former Merck Pharmaceuticals site, citing concerns about utility costs and lack of community input. CoreWeave stated it views data centers as community investments and is committed to engagement.
