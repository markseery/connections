## Summary

**Cloud Infrastructure & Data Centers**

**Anthropic** significantly expanded its compute deal with **Google Cloud** and **Broadcom**, adding 3.5 gigawatts of TPU capacity (coming online in 2027) as part of its $50 billion U.S. compute infrastructure commitment. This follows skyrocketing demand for Claude AI models and Anthropic's recent $30 billion Series G at a $380 billion valuation.

**Firmus**, a Singapore-based data center company backed by **Nvidia**, raised $505 million at a $5.5 billion valuation (led by Coatue), bringing its six-month total to $1.35 billion. Firmus is building energy-efficient "AI factory" data centers in Australia using Nvidia's Vera Rubin next-gen platform (its "Project Southgate"), having pivoted from Bitcoin mining cooling.

**Uber** expanded its **AWS** contract to run more ride-sharing features on Amazon's Graviton chips and began trialing **AWS's Trainium3** AI chip—a notable shift given Uber's previous major cloud deals with **Oracle** and **Google Cloud** in 2023.

**Iran** threatened retaliatory strikes against U.S. tech infrastructure in the Middle East, specifically targeting the **Stargate** data center in the UAE (a $500B joint venture of **OpenAI**, **SoftBank**, and **Oracle**). Iranian missiles had already struck **AWS** data centers in Bahrain and an **Oracle** data center in Dubai.

**SpaceX** is pursuing orbital data centers as part of its potential $1.75 trillion IPO narrative, while **Intel** joined Elon Musk's **Terafab** semiconductor project (alongside SpaceX and Tesla) to build a new U.S. chip fab in Texas, giving Intel's foundry business two major anchor customers.

Insurers are turning to catastrophe bonds to offload growing data center risks. **Poolside**, an AI coding startup, is hunting for new data center partners after its deal with **CoreWeave** fell through.

**AI Models & Products**

**Anthropic** previewed its powerful new frontier model **Mythos** through "Project Glasswing," a cybersecurity initiative with partners including **Amazon**, **Apple**, **Broadcom**, **Cisco**, **CrowdStrike**, **Microsoft**, the Linux Foundation, and Palo Alto Networks. The model reportedly found thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities in software. Anthropic faces tensions with the U.S. Defense Department, which labeled it a supply-chain risk, while the **UK** is courting Anthropic to expand in London.

**Arcee**, a 26-person startup, released its open-source 400B-parameter reasoning model "Trinity Large Thinking" on a $20M budget, positioning it as a Western alternative to Chinese open-source models, and gaining traction with OpenClaw users after Anthropic restricted Claude access for that tool.

**Microsoft** launched a "mid-class" AI model as compute limits bite, while its **Copilot** terms of use controversially state it is "for entertainment purposes only"—language Microsoft called "legacy" and promised to update.

**Google** launched "AI Edge Eloquent," an offline-first Gemma-based dictation app, and added Gemini-powered photo captioning to Google Maps.

**OpenAI** released ChatGPT app integrations (DoorDash, Spotify, Uber, etc.), published policy proposals for the AI economy (public wealth funds, robot taxes, four-day workweek), and acquired a popular tech talk show. Former OpenAI employees launched **Zero Shot**, a new ~$100M VC fund focused on AI.

**Microsoft** launched a "mid-class" AI model responding to compute constraints. **Samsung** forecast record profits driven by the AI boom.

**Investment & Market Trends**

Family offices are increasingly bypassing VCs to invest directly in AI startups, with firms like Arena Private Wealth co-leading a $230M round in AI chip startup **Positron**. The AI gold rush is pulling private wealth into earlier, riskier bets. Spain's **Xoople** raised $130M Series B for AI-focused satellite data. Indian startup **Rocket** launched a platform generating McKinsey-style consulting reports via AI. **Bezos's Project Prometheus** poached an xAI co-founder from OpenAI. The UK is considering testing AI models used by banks.
