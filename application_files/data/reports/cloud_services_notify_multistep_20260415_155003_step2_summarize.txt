## Summary

### Cloud Services & GPU-as-a-Service Providers

**Allbirds pivots to AI cloud computing:** After selling its shoe brand for $39 million, Allbirds is rebranding as **NewBird AI**, a "GPU-as-a-Service and AI-native cloud solutions provider." The company secured $50 million in convertible financing and plans to acquire GPU assets to offer AI compute capacity to customers, with potential future M&A activity. The move leverages its existing Nasdaq-listed public shell (ticker: BIRD). Both TechCrunch and the Financial Times covered this story.

**Parasail raises $32M for inference cloud:** **Parasail**, a cloud computing startup focused on AI inference, raised a $32 million Series A. The company generates 500 billion tokens daily, renting processing time across 40 data centers in 15 countries rather than owning all its own hardware. Founded by former **Groq** executive Mike Henry, Parasail orchestrates workloads across rented GPUs and liquidity markets to drive down inference costs, betting on the proliferation of open-source models and growing demand from developers wanting cheap, fast tokens ("tokenmaxxing").

**Kepler Communications — orbital compute:** Canada's **Kepler Communications** has the largest compute cluster in orbit (~40 Nvidia Orin edge processors on 10 satellites linked by laser). It now has 18 customers, including **Sophia Space**, which will test passively cooled space computer software on Kepler's constellation. Large-scale orbital data centers (envisioned by **SpaceX** and **Blue Origin**) aren't expected until the 2030s.

### Major AI Lab Competition: Anthropic vs. OpenAI

**Anthropic's rapid rise is pressuring OpenAI.** Anthropic's annualized revenue surged from $9 billion (end of 2025) to $30 billion (end of March 2026), driven largely by coding tools. Its $380 billion valuation is increasingly seen as a relative bargain compared to **OpenAI's** $852 billion valuation, which some of OpenAI's own investors now view skeptically. Secondary-market demand for Anthropic shares has grown "nearly insatiable" while OpenAI shares trade at a discount. OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar pointed to its record $122 billion fundraise as evidence of confidence, but one investor compared OpenAI to "the Netscape of AI."

**Anthropic's Mythos model** — deemed too dangerous to release publicly due to powerful cybersecurity capabilities — is generating major waves. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark confirmed the company briefed the **Trump administration** on Mythos. Trump officials (Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed Chair Powell) encouraged major banks — **JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, and Morgan Stanley** — to test Mythos for vulnerability detection. **UK financial regulators** are also rushing to assess its risks. Cybersecurity stocks fell on concerns Anthropic's tool could disrupt the sector. This is all happening while Anthropic is simultaneously suing the Trump DOD over being labeled a supply-chain risk after disputes over military use of its AI.

**OpenAI** acquired personal finance startup **Hiro Finance** (an acqui-hire), released a new cybersecurity model to a limited customer group, and faces investor questions about its strategy and valuation.

### Microsoft

**Microsoft** is testing integration of **OpenClaw-like** local agent features into **Microsoft 365 Copilot** for enterprise customers, with better security controls than the open-source OpenClaw agent. This adds to its growing suite of agentic tools including **Copilot Cowork** (powered partly by Anthropic's **Claude**) and **Copilot Tasks**. The new agent may run locally on user hardware rather than in the cloud.

### Google

**Google** added **AI Skills** to Chrome, letting users save and reuse Gemini AI prompts across web pages. It also expanded **Gemini's Personal Intelligence** feature (connecting Gmail, Photos, YouTube, etc. for personalized answers) to **India** — for AI Pro and AI Ultra users initially, with plans for free users. Google's **TurboQuant** algorithm raised questions about whether it could reduce AI demand for memory chips.

### Adobe

**Adobe** launched the **Firefly AI Assistant** (public beta coming soon), an agentic tool that orchestrates tasks across Photoshop, Premiere, Lightroom, Illustrator, Express, and other Creative Cloud apps. Users direct it via text prompts, buttons, and sliders, with the assistant learning creative preferences over time and offering multi-step "skills" (e.g., adapting images for social media platforms).

### Apple

**Apple** is blocking/removing **vibe-coding apps** (Replit, Vibecode, Anything) from the App Store over code-execution policy concerns. Separately, Apple is reportedly testing four designs for **smart glasses** (planned for 2027) — no displays, but with camera, calls, music, and Siri integration, similar to Meta's Ray-Ban glasses.

### Data Center Regulation

**Maine** became the first U.S. state to pass a **data center construction ban**, reflecting growing public pushback against energy-hungry AI infrastructure.

### Tokenmaxxing Debate

The practice of tracking employee AI token usage ("tokenmaxxing") as a productivity metric is generating debate. **Meta** shut down its internal tokenmaxxing dashboard after it leaked. **Reid Hoffman** endorsed the concept at Semafor's summit, saying companies should track employee token spend to encourage AI adoption. Critics argue it's a flawed productivity measure.

### AI Industry Sentiment & Society

A **Stanford** annual report found growing disconnect between AI insiders and the public, with rising anxiety about jobs, medical care, and the economy — especially among Gen Z. The public backlash has been visible in reactions to attacks on Sam Altman's home (a **molotov cocktail** was thrown). AI chatbots were found to **misdiagnose in over 80% of early medical cases**. The AI build-out is powering global goods trade, and AI-generated legal queries are pushing up lawyers' fees. **Meta** built an AI version of Mark Zuckerberg to interact with staff. Short seller Carson Block sees opportunity in AI disruption.

### Other Notable Items

- **Gitar** emerged from stealth with $9M to deploy AI agents for code validation/security — addressing "code overload" from vibe coding.
- **Vercel** CEO signaled IPO readiness as ARR hit $340M run rate, fueled by AI-generated app explosion.
- **Science Corporation** (from ex-Neuralink co-founder Max Hodak) is preparing first human brain-computer interface trials, backed by a $230M Series C at $1.5B valuation.
- **Forum AI** (co-founded by ex-Meta/CNN's Campbell Brown) is working on making AI platforms more trustworthy.
- Democrats fear Big Tech lobby groups as AI regulatory fights loom.
