<td style="padding:20px 48px 0 48px;">
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Kepler Communications</strong> launched the largest orbital compute cluster — about 40 Nvidia Orin edge processors across 10 satellites linked by laser communications — and now has 18 customers, including <strong>Sophia Space</strong>, which will test passively-cooled space-computer software on the constellation. Large-scale orbital data centers (envisioned by <strong>SpaceX</strong> and <strong>Blue Origin</strong>) are not expected until the 2030s. Kepler sees itself as infrastructure for space and airborne applications, not a data center company.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Google Cloud and Intel</strong> expanded a multiyear partnership: Google Cloud will continue using Intel's Xeon 6 processors for AI, cloud, and inference, and the two will co-develop custom ASIC-based infrastructure processing units (IPUs) for data center workloads. The deal reflects growing industry demand for CPUs alongside GPUs. <strong>Arm Holdings</strong> (SoftBank-owned) also recently unveiled its own AGI CPU to address a worldwide CPU shortage. Arm chief Rene Haas is reportedly in line to lead much of SoftBank's international business.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Amazon CEO Andy Jassy's</strong> annual shareholder letter positioned <strong>AWS's</strong> homegrown <strong>Trainium</strong> AI chips as a challenger to <strong>Nvidia</strong>, claiming Trainium has hit a $20 billion ARR (and would be $50B if sold externally). Demand for Trainium3 and even the still-distant Trainium4 is nearly sold out. Jassy also took implicit aim at <strong>Intel</strong>, <strong>Starlink</strong>, and others across Amazon's businesses.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>OpenAI halted its Stargate UK data centre project</strong>, according to the Financial Times.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Google's TurboQuant</strong> algorithm raised questions about whether it could reduce AI demand for memory chips.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
The <strong>AI build-out is broadly powering global goods trade</strong>, per the FT, as hardware shipments for data centers surge worldwide.

<td style="padding:20px 48px 0 48px;">
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Anthropic's new Mythos model</strong> dominated headlines. It was released in a limited fashion to major enterprises (<strong>AWS, JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley</strong>) because of its exceptional ability to find software security vulnerabilities. <strong>Trump administration officials</strong> (Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed Chair Powell) summoned bank executives to encourage testing Mythos for cyber defense. <strong>UK financial regulators</strong> are also assessing Mythos risks. Cybersecurity stocks fell on worries the model could disrupt the sector. Critics argued the limited release may be more about enterprise sales strategy and preventing model distillation by competitors than about safety alone. Anthropic is simultaneously battling the Trump administration in court over a DoD supply-chain risk designation.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Anthropic is closing in on OpenAI</strong> in US business usage, per the FT. At the <strong>HumanX conference</strong>, attendees and vendors consistently named <strong>Claude</strong> as the preferred AI chatbot, with many saying <strong>ChatGPT/OpenAI</strong> had "fallen off." OpenAI recently dropped side projects (Sora, a "sexy" ChatGPT variant) to refocus on business and coding, but negative press, advertising in ChatGPT, and the Trump administration partnership have hurt perception.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Anthropic temporarily banned OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger</strong> (now at OpenAI) from Claude access, citing "suspicious" activity. The ban was reversed after it went viral. This followed Anthropic's new policy requiring OpenClaw users to pay via API rather than subscriptions — a "claw tax" — citing compute-intensive agentic usage patterns. Steinberger suggested this was a competitive move to benefit Anthropic's own <strong>Cowork</strong> agent.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>OpenAI launched a new $100/month ChatGPT Pro plan</strong> to compete directly with Anthropic's $100/month Claude tier, offering 5x more access to its <strong>Codex</strong> coding tool than the $20 Plus plan. The $200 tier remains available with 20x limits. Plans now range from free (with ads), $8 Go (with ads), $20 Plus, $100 Pro, and $200 Pro.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Meta's Muse Spark</strong> model — the first release under <strong>Alexandr Wang</strong> (recruited from <strong>Scale AI</strong> to lead Meta's Superintelligence Labs) — pushed the <strong>Meta AI app to #5 on the US App Store</strong> (87% daily download increase on iOS). Muse Spark accepts multimodal input, supports subagents, visual coding, and will expand to WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Messenger, and Meta's AI glasses. Separately, <strong>Meta built an AI version of Mark Zuckerberg</strong> to interact with staff internally. Meta paused contracts with <strong>Mercor</strong> after that startup's data breach.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>Alibaba</strong> is shifting its AI strategy toward revenue generation over open-source, per the FT.
</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;font-size:15px;line-height:1.7;color:#333333;">
<strong>xAI</strong> (Elon Musk's company) sued Colorado over the first state AI anti-discrimination law.
</p>

<strong>A stalking victim sued OpenAI</strong>, alleging ChatGPT fueled her abuser's delusions and that OpenAI ignored three warnings (including an internal mass-casualty flag). </p>
