# Summary: Cloud Services Providers in the AI Infrastructure Boom

## CoreWeave Dominates Headlines

**CoreWeave** (NASDAQ: CRWV) is at the center of a massive AI infrastructure funding wave. In rapid succession, the neocloud provider secured several landmark deals:

- **Meta Platforms** committed an additional **$21 billion** (on top of a prior $14.2B deal) for AI cloud infrastructure running through December 2032, bringing Meta's total commitment to over **$35 billion**. The deal supports Meta's next-gen AI models and agentic AI assistants, including early deployment of **NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform**.
- **Anthropic** signed a multi-year agreement for CoreWeave to power its **Claude AI models**, with a phased deployment using various NVIDIA chip architectures. This means **nine of the top ten AI model providers** now use CoreWeave's platform (the exception being Elon Musk's xAI).
- CoreWeave simultaneously raised **~$3.5 billion in convertible senior notes**, secured an **$8.5 billion delayed draw term loan** at investment-grade ratings, and upsized a high-yield bond offering — demonstrating aggressive capital formation to fund expansion.

CoreWeave's stock surged over 10% on these announcements. Its **revenue backlog has ballooned to ~$66.8–$87.8 billion**, with 2025 revenue up 168% YoY. The company carries over **$21 billion in debt** but is using it to scale rapidly. Bank of America raised its price target to **$120** with a Buy rating; Macquarie upgraded to Outperform; D.A. Davidson raised its target to **$175**. Despite bullish momentum, some analysts (Bernstein, Mizuho) remain cautious, noting low profitability scores and insider selling.

**NVIDIA** is deeply intertwined with CoreWeave — as investor ($2B+ invested), supplier of GPUs, and customer (pledging $6.3B+ in services through 2032). This circular relationship gives CoreWeave early access to the latest GPU platforms.

**Microsoft** was CoreWeave's first major customer (2023), unique since Azure is the second-largest cloud platform. The partnership likely ensures OpenAI has sufficient compute capacity.

## Nebius Gaining Ground

**Nebius Group** (NASDAQ: NB), another NVIDIA-backed company, is emerging as a major competitor. It recently secured a **$27 billion deal with Meta Platforms** — one of the largest in the industry. Bank of America raised Nebius's price target from **$150 to $175** with a Buy rating, citing strong AI infrastructure demand.

## IREN Under Pressure

**IREN** has rebounded from $30 to $40 but remains down 50% from its 2025 highs. While it holds a **$9.7 billion Microsoft partnership**, it has not landed major new hyperscaler deals. Its debt has surged to over **$3.6 billion**, and a **$6 billion ATM equity offering** to fund 150K GPU purchases will dilute shareholders. Short interest has risen to **16%**. Competitors including **Riot Platforms, Mara Holdings, and Cipher Mining** are entering the AI data center space. Analysts still expect strong revenue growth (~97% YoY), and GPU rental prices have risen 40% in 60 days.

## Broader Cloud and AI Market Context

- **Hyperscalers** (Meta, Google, Microsoft, Amazon) continue building their own data centers while simultaneously relying on neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nebius for overflow capacity. Meta alone plans a **$10 billion Texas data center**.
- **Anthropic** — heavily backed by **Amazon (AWS)** and **Alphabet (Google)** — chose CoreWeave's bare-metal architecture over traditional clouds for an estimated **20–30% boost in inference throughput**, signaling a "performance-first" procurement shift.
- **Traditional cloud providers** (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) face growing competition from specialized neoclouds for AI-specific workloads, though they remain dominant in broader cloud services.
- Creditors are becoming more comfortable financing neoclouds, with CoreWeave achieving its first **investment-grade facility at ~6% interest**.
- The market is shifting from AI model training toward **large-scale inference and agentic AI applications**, intensifying demand for specialized compute infrastructure.
- Geopolitical risks (Middle East tensions, energy-driven inflation) and a hawkish Federal Reserve are adding volatility to the broader market backdrop.
