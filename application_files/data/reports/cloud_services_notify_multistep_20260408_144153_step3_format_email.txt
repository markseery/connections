<h2>CoreWeave: Rapid Growth, High Risk, Strategic Shifts</h2>

<p><strong>CoreWeave (CRWV)</strong> remains one of the most talked-about AI cloud infrastructure plays. The company, which IPO'd in March 2025 at $40/share, reported <strong>2025 revenue of $5.1 billion</strong> (up 167% YoY), with Q4 revenue of $1.57 billion (up 110% YoY). Despite explosive top-line growth, CoreWeave <strong>operated at a loss</strong> for Q4 and the full year due to massive infrastructure spending. Its contractual backlog reached <strong>$67 billion</strong>, with major customers including <strong>OpenAI and Meta</strong>. The company's market cap stands at approximately <strong>$39–41 billion</strong>.</p>

<p>CoreWeave secured an <strong>$8.5 billion GPU-backed loan facility</strong> (backed by a long-term Meta contract), which sent its stock up 12%. This financing enabled a strategic pivot away from fixed, single-tenant commitments toward <strong>flexible multi-partner leasing</strong>. As a direct consequence, CoreWeave and AI startup <strong>Poolside mutually terminated</strong> their agreement for "Project Horizon," a planned <strong>2-gigawatt data center campus in West Texas</strong>. Poolside — which had planned CoreWeave as anchor tenant for a 15-year, 250 MW first phase with 40,000+ NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 GPUs — is now seeking new partners, reportedly in talks with <strong>Google</strong> and other cloud providers. A planned Nvidia-backed funding round for Poolside also collapsed.</p>

<div class="highlight-box">
<p><strong>Insider activity</strong> raised eyebrows: CoreWeave's CEO and CSO sold <strong>$146 million in stock</strong> shortly after the IPO. SEC filings reveal multiple Rule 144 sales in early 2026, including a Fidelity Charitable sale of 303,929 shares for ~$23 million. <strong>Coatue Management</strong> (billionaire Philippe Laffont's hedge fund), which led CoreWeave's Series C in 2024 and once held it as its largest position, <strong>fully exited its stake</strong> in Q4 — timing that preceded a ~50% share price decline from October highs.</p>
</div>

<p>CoreWeave's stock currently trades around <strong>$85</strong>, well below its 52-week high of $187 but above the $40 IPO price — up ~7.5% YTD and ~62% over one year. The company owes <strong>$21 billion in debt</strong>, and customer concentration is flagged as a key risk. Analysts are divided: <strong>Bernstein</strong> maintains a <strong>Sell</strong> rating with a $56 target; <strong>Barclays</strong> rates it <strong>Hold</strong> at $90; <strong>Northland Securities</strong> has a <strong>Buy</strong>; and the consensus is <strong>"Moderate Buy."</strong> Cathie Wood's ARK continues buying shares.</p>

<p>CoreWeave is also expanding its customer base beyond hyperscalers: <strong>Cline</strong> selected CoreWeave for autonomous engineering workloads, and <strong>Zonos</strong> chose it for cross-border commerce AI. The company now offers <strong>NVIDIA HGX B300</strong> (Blackwell) at scale, earned <strong>SemiAnalysis' Platinum ClusterMAX rating</strong> twice, and is pushing aggressively into <strong>AI inference</strong> alongside training.</p>

<h2>Nebius: The Challenger Gaining Ground</h2>

<p><strong>Nebius (NBIS)</strong> has emerged as a serious CoreWeave competitor, with its stock up <strong>~400% in 12 months</strong> (vs. CoreWeave's ~109%). It secured a landmark <strong>$27 billion, five-year AI infrastructure deal with Meta</strong> ($12B dedicated + $15B option) and a <strong>$2 billion strategic investment from Nvidia</strong> to deploy 5+ GW of Nvidia systems through 2030. Nebius also holds a deal with <strong>Microsoft worth up to $19.4 billion</strong>.</p>

<p>Q4 revenue surged <strong>547% YoY to $228 million</strong> (though still far smaller than CoreWeave's $1.57B). Nebius had 170 MW of live capacity at end-2025 but plans to reach <strong>800 MW–1 GW by end of 2026</strong> and is building a 300 MW data center in Finland, targeting <strong>3 GW of contracted business</strong>. It raised <strong>$4 billion in convertible notes</strong>. Like CoreWeave, Nebius is <strong>not yet profitable</strong> (Q4 net loss: $173M).</p>

<div class="highlight-box">
<p>Both CoreWeave and Nebius have <strong>outperformed every "Magnificent Seven" stock in 2026</strong> (Nvidia -5%, Apple -6%, Alphabet -3%, Microsoft -23%, Amazon -8%, Meta -13%, Tesla -23%), with Nebius up 30%+ and CoreWeave up 15%+ YTD.</p>
</div>

<h2>Nvidia's Central Role</h2>

<p><strong>Nvidia</strong> sits at the nexus of the AI cloud buildout. It owns <strong>&gt;12% of CoreWeave</strong> and has made a <strong>$2 billion investment in Nebius</strong> — effectively betting on both leading neoclouds. Nvidia's own revenue grew 65% in fiscal 2026, its market cap is near <strong>$4.1 trillion</strong>, and it trades at a P/E of 35. GPU rental prices are reportedly <strong>up 40% since October</strong>, reflecting sustained demand. However, Nvidia's massive scale makes future exponential stock growth harder, which is partly why investors are looking at CoreWeave and Nebius as higher-growth alternatives.</p>

<h2>Broader Cloud &amp; AI Infrastructure Context</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>AWS</strong>: Experienced a fire at a data center in Bahrain (reportedly following an Iranian attack). Separately, <strong>Uber expanded its AWS partnership</strong> to build AI capabilities.</li>
<li><strong>Microsoft</strong>: Announced a <strong>$10 billion investment in AI and cybersecurity in Japan</strong>. Also party to Nebius's up-to-$19.4B deal.</li>
<li><strong>Google</strong>: In talks with Poolside as a potential new partner for its Texas data center project after the CoreWeave deal fell through.</li>
<li><strong>Meta</strong>: A central customer driving the AI infrastructure boom, with massive contracts powering both CoreWeave ($8.5B loan backed by Meta contract) and Nebius ($27B deal).</li>
<li><strong>Iren</strong>: Stock <strong>plunged 20% in 5 days</strong>; Freedom Capital issued a Hold rating.</li>
<li>AI compute rental prices are <strong>up 40%</strong> since October, underscoring strong demand across the sector.</li>
<li>The AI data center sector is shifting toward <strong>capital flexibility</strong> — companies are moving away from long-term single-tenant commitments toward diversified, multi-partner models.</li>
<li>Michael Burry's $1.1 billion bet against AI is reportedly performing well as some AI stocks decline.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>

<div class="takeaway-box">
<p>The AI cloud infrastructure race is intensifying. <strong style="color:#ffffff;">CoreWeave</strong> leads in scale (43 data centers, 850 MW, $5.1B revenue) but carries significant debt and customer concentration risk. <strong style="color:#ffffff;">Nebius</strong> is growing faster from a smaller base with elite backing from both Nvidia and Meta. Both remain unprofitable as they spend aggressively on GPU capacity and data centers. Traditional hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) continue their own massive buildouts. Investor sentiment is mixed — enormous demand for AI compute persists, but profitability timelines, leverage levels, and insider selling warrant caution.</p>
