# Summary: CoreWeave and the AI Cloud Infrastructure Landscape

## CoreWeave's Major Deals and Partnerships

**CoreWeave** (NASDAQ: CRWV) has been at the center of a flurry of AI infrastructure deal-making:

- **Meta** expanded its commitment to CoreWeave by $21 billion (on top of a prior $14.2 billion arrangement), covering 2027–2032, as Meta supplements its own data center buildout with CoreWeave's GPU capacity. Meta also separately announced a $10 billion Texas data center.
- **Anthropic** signed a multi-year agreement with CoreWeave to power its Claude AI models, sending CoreWeave shares up ~10%.
- **Jane Street** signed a $6 billion AI cloud services agreement with CoreWeave.
- **OpenAI** remains a key customer. CoreWeave is purchasing GPUs and related components to fulfill OpenAI contracts, funded in part by new debt.
- CoreWeave emphasized its diversified customer base, listing **Meta, Anthropic, Microsoft, Google, IBM, Perplexity AI, Jane Street**, and others.

## Financing and Capital Raises

- **Morgan Stanley** and **Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group** kicked off a ~$3.1 billion leveraged loan for CoreWeave to buy GPUs, adding to $2.75 billion raised earlier in the month.
- CoreWeave raised $3.5 billion in convertible notes and priced $1 billion of 9.750% senior notes due 2031.
- CoreWeave's SUNK (runtime acceleration) platform expanded capabilities, including "SUNK Anywhere" for multi-cloud environments, aimed at getting AI clusters online faster.

## OpenAI's Missed Targets and Market Fallout

A **Wall Street Journal** report revealed that **OpenAI** missed its internal targets for both annual revenue and its goal of 1 billion weekly ChatGPT users by year-end. **Google's Gemini** AI bot grew and ate into OpenAI's market share. This sparked a sell-off in AI infrastructure stocks:

- **Oracle** (ORCL) fell ~4%, **CoreWeave** ~5%, and **AMD** and **Nvidia** also declined.
- **Oracle** defended its OpenAI partnership on X, expressing continued excitement. CoreWeave noted OpenAI is "a terrific partner, but not our only one."
- Analysts warned that OpenAI's slowing growth should still concern **Oracle** and **CoreWeave** investors given their heavy spending on AI capacity.

## Nebius

**Nebius Group** (NasdaqGS: NBIS) is in active talks to acquire Israeli AI firm **AI21 Labs** (after Nvidia exited earlier negotiations). Nebius is also expanding its AI infrastructure through new data center capacity contracts with **CoreWeave**. The company positions itself as a full-stack AI cloud competitor to **AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud**. Nebius shares were up 61% YTD and ~7x over one year, trading at ~$145.

## Galaxy Digital and Data Centers

**Galaxy Digital** narrowed its Q1 net loss to $216 million and delivered its first data hall at its Helios campus in Texas to **CoreWeave**, beginning revenue under a long-term AI workload lease. Galaxy secured approval to expand Helios power capacity to over 1.6 GW.

## Analyst Activity and Stock Movements

- **Wells Fargo** reiterated CoreWeave as overweight, raising its price target to $135 from $125, calling it the leading "pick-and-shovel" AI infrastructure play.
- **Vanguard Group** purchased CoreWeave shares; **Cathie Wood** (ARK Invest) was reported buying CoreWeave, Spotify, and Google.
- CoreWeave insiders sold significant stock (~$40.9 million and 200,000 shares in separate transactions).
- CoreWeave's stock showed extreme volatility—down over 60% from its $187 high at its worst, then rallying 61% from late-March lows. It traded around $105–$112 in late April.
- The company reports Q1 2026 earnings on May 7.
- Valuation debates intensified: CoreWeave has a 47.5% EBITDA margin and ~$5.1 billion revenue but remains net-income negative (EPS of -$0.56). Analysts are split on whether it is overvalued or a growth opportunity.

## Broader Cloud/AI Competition Context

- **Google's** Gemini is gaining ground against ChatGPT.
- **Microsoft, Google, Meta**, and other hyperscalers continue building their own AI data centers while also relying on neocloud providers like CoreWeave and Nebius for supplemental capacity.
- Concerns persist about AI sector overspending and whether demand will justify the massive infrastructure buildout.
