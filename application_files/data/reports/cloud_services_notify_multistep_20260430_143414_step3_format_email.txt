<p>Q1 2026 Earnings &amp; AI Developments</p>

<div class="content">

<div class="section">
<h2 class="section-title">Big Tech Cloud &amp; AI Earnings (Q1 2026)</h2>

<div class="card">
<h3>AWS (Amazon)</h3>
<p>
Net sales hit <span class="stat">$37.6B</span> <span class="stat">+28% YoY</span> — its fastest growth in 15 quarters. CEO Andy Jassy attributed the surge to AI demand, noting AWS's AI revenue run rate now exceeds <strong>$15 billion</strong>. Amazon warned that capital expenditure will keep rising as it invests in land, power, buildings, chips, and networking. AWS also <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/28/amazon-is-already-offering-new-openai-products-on-aws/">immediately began offering OpenAI products</a> (models, Codex, and a new "Bedrock Managed Agents" service) after Microsoft lost its exclusivity over OpenAI's technology.
</p>
</div>

<div class="card">
<h3>Google Cloud</h3>
<p>
Surpassed <span class="stat">$20B quarterly revenue</span> <span class="stat">+63% YoY</span> for the first time. GenAI-based products grew ~800% YoY and API token usage hit 16 billion per minute. However, Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai acknowledged growth was <strong>capacity-constrained</strong> — <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/google-cloud-surpasses-20b-but-says-growth-was-capacity-constrained/">cloud backlog doubled to $462 billion</a> — and said revenue would have been higher if demand could be met. Google is <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/2138e81c-4d86-46f4-8ca0-287f8b737cdf">outpacing rivals</a> in planned AI spending, with Big Tech's combined AI capex plans rising to an estimated <strong>$725 billion</strong>.
</p>
</div>

<div class="card">
<h3>Microsoft</h3>
<p>
AI business exceeded a <span class="stat">$37B annual run rate</span> <span class="stat">+123% YoY</span>. CEO <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/satya-nadella-says-hes-ready-to-exploit-the-new-openai-deal/">Satya Nadella said</a> the revised OpenAI deal — which ends Microsoft's exclusivity but gives it royalty-free access to OpenAI's frontier models through 2032 — is still favorable. He plans to "fully exploit" that IP access. <strong>Microsoft 365 Copilot</strong> now has over <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/microsoft-says-it-has-over-20m-paid-copilot-users-and-they-really-are-using-it/">20 million paid enterprise seats</a>, with weekly engagement matching Outlook usage levels. Copilot queries per user rose ~20% QoQ, and major deals include Accenture (740,000+ seats). Nadella emphasized Copilot is multi-model, also supporting Anthropic's Claude.
</p>
</div>

<div class="card">
<h3>Meta</h3>
<p>
Posted <span class="stat">$56.3B revenue</span> <span class="stat">+33% YoY</span> and $26.8 billion net income (+61%). Its <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/meta-says-its-business-ai-now-facilitates-10-million-conversations-a-week/">business AI tools hit 10 million conversations/week</a> (up from 1 million in early 2026), powered by the new Muse Spark LLM. Over 8 million advertisers use its genAI ad creative tools. Meta raised its 2026 capex forecast to <strong>$125–$145 billion</strong>, largely due to higher memory component costs. <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/meta-is-still-burning-money-on-ar-vr/">Reality Labs continued losing ~$4 billion/quarter</a> ($83.5 billion cumulative since 2021). <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/1e4c269a-5258-406c-a308-e55c3d5d640f">China blocked Meta's $2 billion acquisition of AI group Manus</a>.
</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2 class="section-title">OpenAI / Microsoft / Amazon Realignment</h2>
<div class="card">
<h3>Partnership Restructured</h3>
<p>
<a href="https://www.ft.com/content/20e63d1d-835f-4397-ae88-e7097be1e503">OpenAI and Microsoft formally loosened their exclusive partnership</a>. Microsoft retains royalty-free access to OpenAI's most advanced AI through 2032 but no longer has exclusivity. <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/f159dd74-56a5-404b-ae54-ab4bab98b2c3">OpenAI immediately expanded its Amazon/AWS relationship</a>, making models, Codex, and AI agent tools available on AWS Bedrock. Microsoft, meanwhile, is deepening ties with <strong>Anthropic</strong> for agent products powered by Claude.
</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2 class="section-title">Anthropic's Massive Fundraise</h2>
<div class="card">
<h3>Up to $50B at ~$900B Valuation</h3>
<p>
<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/sources-anthropic-could-raise-a-new-50b-round-at-a-valuation-of-900b/">Anthropic has received multiple preemptive offers</a> to raise <strong>~$40–50 billion</strong> at an <span class="stat">$850–900B valuation</span>, potentially its last private round before an IPO. Its annual revenue run rate has surged past $30 billion (up from ~$9 billion at end of 2025), driven heavily by AI coding products (Claude Code, Cowork). A board decision on the round is expected in May. <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/aa3a7a19-ab94-4069-aea4-e192ab9abc41">Goldman Sachs separately stopped bankers from using Claude in Hong Kong</a>.
</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2 class="section-title">SoftBank's Data Center Robotics Venture</h2>
<div class="card">
<h3>Roze AI — Autonomous Data Center Construction</h3>
<p>
<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/softbank-is-creating-a-robotics-company-that-builds-data-centers-and-already-eyeing-a-100b-ipo/">SoftBank is creating Roze AI</a>, a new company that would deploy autonomous robots to build data centers more efficiently in the U.S. The conglomerate is already eyeing a <span class="stat">$100B IPO</span> potentially in H2 2026, though some insiders are skeptical about the valuation and timeline. The FT also <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/55c7d99c-7e68-453c-b784-33d6b9838e16">reported on SoftBank's plans to list the new AI and robotics company in the US</a>.
</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="section">
<h2 class="section-title">Infrastructure &amp; Data Center Themes</h2>
<div class="card">
<h3>The AI Infrastructure Buildout</h3>
<p>
The FT explored <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/ba4e8e02-11e9-437c-b6a9-89778647a6cc">the "great American data centre divide,"</a> while Big Tech collectively planned <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/2138e81c-4d86-46f4-8ca0-287f8b737cdf">$725 billion in AI infrastructure spending</a>. Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon are all aggressively expanding capacity, with Google explicitly noting it is compute-constrained. <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/620c3442-ee5a-41c6-b6db-9457e1adcd7e">Memory chipmakers hope AI demand will end their traditional boom-and-bust cycles</a>.
</p>
</div>
</div>
